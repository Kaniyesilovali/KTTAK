Child-friendly space setup protocol

In the first days after a disaster, a child-friendly space gives a child a predictable day and a safe adult. This protocol covers site selection, protection standards, the daily session and the exit plan.

Protocol 14 min read For setup teams CC BY-NC 4.0

Purpose

What a child-friendly space does

  • Protects children from physical danger and from the risk of abuse
  • Restores a predictable structure to the day
  • Provides age-appropriate play, learning and contact with peers
  • Gives caregivers hours in which their child is safe and they can act
  • Allows children at risk to be noticed early and referred
  • Runs with the community, using the community's own capacity

Limits

What it is not

  • Not a nursery or a childminding service
  • Not a replacement for school, and it must not delay the return to school
  • Not a therapy centre; no group therapy takes place
  • Not a permanent institution — it opens with an exit plan already written
  • Not a showcase for donor visits and photographs
  • Not something an untrained volunteer can run alone

Step 1

Choosing the site

The decision to open is taken together with the community itself. Before choosing a site, ask families and children: where can they safely go, which route is risky, which hours work?

Structural safety

  • An undamaged building or a tent in the open; a damaged building is never used
  • Away from rubble, pits, traffic and water channels
  • A safe distance from power lines and generators
  • Level, non-slip ground with no sharp material

Access

  • Within walking distance, along a safe route
  • Accessible by wheelchair
  • Equal access for girls and boys
  • Open to all communities, with no discrimination

Facilities

  • Clean water and a handwashing point
  • Separate, lockable, lit toilets
  • Shade, warmth and ventilation
  • A single supervised entrance and a defined perimeter

A practical measure for size: roughly 3 m² of indoor space per child, and more in the outdoor play area. A crowded space stops being a safe one; if you are over capacity, move to sessions in shifts.

Step 2

Protection standards — not negotiable

Staffing and ratios

  • At least two adults with every group; no adult is ever alone with a child
  • Ratios commonly used in the field: one facilitator to 15 children over six, and one to five for under-sixes
  • Women and men facilitators work together in the team
  • Facilitators are recruited from the community and trained
  • Additional support staff are planned when disabled children attend

Child protection rules

  • Every worker and volunteer signs the child protection policy; references and background checks are carried out
  • Entry and exit are recorded; a child is released only to a registered caregiver or a person notified in advance
  • Photography and filming are prohibited; any exception requires the informed consent of the child and caregiver and organisational approval
  • The reporting route is displayed where everyone can see it: who to tell, how, and within what time
  • An unaccompanied child is reported to the protection team immediately and never left alone in the space

If any one of these cannot be met, the space does not open. A child-friendly space set up with a missing protection standard is more dangerous than no space at all, because it leads everyone to assume that protection is in place.

Step 3

The daily session — three hours

The sequence is itself the intervention: a day that repeats in the same order tells a child that the world is predictable again. Do not change the order; if you must, say so in advance.

  • 15 min

    Welcome and registration

    Greeting by name, sign-in, handwashing. Having the same facilitator at the door strengthens the effect of a familiar face.

  • 15 min

    Opening circle

    The plan for the day is explained, the rules of the space are repeated together, a name game is played. The rules are written with the children and stay on the wall.

  • 45 min

    Structured activity

    Work in age groups: drawing, music, movement games, stories. No activity asks a child to retell the traumatic event.

  • 20 min

    Break — water and a snack

    Toilet and handwashing. Allergy information must be on record; distribution is equal and visible.

  • 45 min

    Free play

    Play the child chooses. The facilitator observes rather than directs. Play that re-enacts the earthquake is not stopped; it is allowed within the bounds of safety.

  • 20 min

    Settling

    A slow close: breathing games, a quiet story, relaxation. No child should leave the space in a state of high arousal.

  • 20 min

    Closing and handover

    Tomorrow's plan is announced — which makes the parting easier. The release record is signed. The team then holds a short 20-minute review.

Step 4

Materials

  • Cushions, matting or rugs; shade cover
  • Crayons, paper, blunt scissors, glue
  • Balls, rope, light sports equipment
  • Building blocks, puzzles, puppets
  • Story books — in Turkish and other languages
  • Register, name badges, a wall board
  • First aid kit and hand sanitiser
  • A lockable materials cupboard

Step 5

Team training

  • Child protection policy and reporting route
  • Basic psychological first aid
  • Running age-appropriate activities
  • Including disabled children
  • Handling discrimination and exclusion
  • Record keeping and confidentiality
  • Weekly supervision meeting

Step 6

Monitoring and exit

  • Daily attendance by age and gender
  • Follow-up on children who stop coming
  • Regular feedback from children and families
  • Number of referrals and their outcome
  • Hours shifting around the school day as schools reopen
  • A closing date and handover plan written from the start
  • Handover to the community: who continues, with what resources

Printable

Pre-opening checklist

Site:    Date:    Responsible:

Site

  • Building undamaged, or open site is safe
  • Single supervised entrance, perimeter defined
  • Accessible by wheelchair
  • Clean water and handwashing point
  • Separate, lockable, lit toilets
  • Shade / warmth provided
  • Roughly 3 m² per child

Team and protection

  • At least two adults per group
  • Women and men facilitators together
  • Everyone has signed the child protection policy
  • References and background checks done
  • Reporting route displayed on the wall
  • Entry, exit and release records ready
  • Photography ban announced to staff and visitors

Operation

  • Daily session written up and on the board
  • Rules of the space written with the children
  • Age groups and shift plan set
  • Allergy and health information recorded
  • First aid kit in place and stocked
  • Referral list current (health, protection, MHPSS)

Community and exit

  • Site and hours chosen in consultation with the community
  • Families know the registration and release rule
  • Feedback and complaints channel announced
  • Closing date and handover plan in writing
  • Hours aligned with school reopening
