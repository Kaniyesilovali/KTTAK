Our working model: the risk and response cycle

How TAK-DER approaches disasters and crises: five stages from identifying risk to recovery.

  1. 2.2.1 Identifying risk
  2. 2.2.2 Prevention and risk reduction
  3. 2.2.3 Preparedness
  4. 2.2.4 Psychological first aid during an event
  5. 2.2.5 Recovery and reconstruction

2.2.1

Identifying risk

We identify which hazards on the island could affect whom, how and to what extent, and in particular map vulnerable groups and existing sources of support.

Starting points: the Cyprus risk profile (earthquake, forest fire, flood, violence) and vulnerable groups (children, older people, people with disabilities, migrants, responders). A detailed analysis of each hazard will be written together with the relevant institutions.

Priority 1 — Understanding disaster risk

Tools

  • Psychosocial needs assessment form

2.2.2

Prevention and risk reduction

Although we cannot always prevent disasters and crises, we inform the public, institutions and the media and carry out protective work to reduce their psychological impact.

For the media: responsible reporting guide. For the public: misinformation and verification.

Sendai

Priorities 2 and 3 — Strengthening risk governance; investing in risk reduction for resilience

Tools

  • Guide for the media

2.2.3

Preparedness

So that institutions, schools and families are ready when a crisis strikes, we provide psychological first aid training and keep our volunteer teams prepared.

For institutions

  • Needs assessment form
  • Child-friendly space

For schools

  • School preparedness curriculum

For families

  • Family disaster plan
  • Emergency kit
  • Home safety

Sendai

Priority 4 — Preparedness for effective response

Tools

  • Training and calendar
  • Volunteer teams

2.2.4

Psychological first aid during an event

During an event and in the first days we support safety, access to accurate information and social connection, and refer those who need more support to appropriate services.

When an event occurs, an incident page is opened on the site from a prepared template: verified information, where to get support, what to do, and updates.

Sendai

Priority 4 — Preparedness for effective response

Tools

  • Psychological first aid
  • Child-friendly space
  • Misinformation and verification

2.2.4 · For whom, how

Psychological first aid by age group and audience

A separate guide is being prepared for each audience. Those that are ready are linked; the rest will be added as they are completed.

  • ChildrenTalking by age, a sense of safety.
  • Young peoplePeers, social media, school.
  • AdultsLook, listen, link.
  • ParentsChildren’s reactions, routines at home.
  • TeachersBack to class, school-based support.
  • JournalistsResponsible reporting, protecting yourself.
  • Health and field workersSecondary trauma, team support.
  • Other vulnerable groupsOlder people, people with disabilities, migrants.

2.2.5

Recovery and reconstruction

Recovery takes months and years, not weeks. This stage focuses on follow-up, grief support, referring people with long-lasting reactions to specialist care and looking after responders. “Build Back Better” applies not only to buildings but also to social ties and services.

Sendai

Priority 4 — “Build Back Better” in recovery

Tools

  • After a loss: on grief
  • Sleep, startle and anxiety
  • Secondary trauma

A common language

Alignment with the Sendai Framework

TAK-DER’s work aligns with the four priorities of the UN Office for Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR) Sendai Framework: understanding disaster risk; strengthening disaster risk governance; investing in risk reduction for resilience; and enhancing preparedness for effective response and to “build back better”. The framework gives us a common language with national and international partners.
