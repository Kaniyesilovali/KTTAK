9.1

Responsible reporting guide

Eight core principles for reporting on disaster, crisis and trauma.

Verify first

Death tolls, numbers injured and the cause of an event are reported only from official sources and after verification. Uncertainty is stated openly.

Consent and dignity

Before speaking to someone in shock, say who you are and where the story will appear; respect a “no”.

Children

Children’s faces, names and identifying details are not published; interviews with children happen only with an adult present and with consent.

Choosing images

Images of bodies, severe injury and intense suffering re-traumatise. Avoid using the same image over and over.

Reporting on suicide

No details of method or location, no sensational headlines, never present suicide as a “solution” — and always add information on where to find help.

Help information

Every story includes where to get support: 112, the relevant institutions and verified aid channels.

Avoiding stigma

Prefer “affected people” or “survivors” to “victims”; do not present psychological reactions as illness.

The reporter too

Journalists working in disaster areas also carry a risk of secondary trauma. Editors should plan rotation and support.
