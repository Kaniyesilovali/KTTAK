Social and economic crises

Job loss, debt, epidemics, migration or social tension: crises that build up over months rather than in a moment, quietly wearing families down.

On this page

  • What it is
  • Expected reactions
  • What you can do
  • When to seek support
  • References

01

What it is

The psychological impact of social and economic crises is often less visible than a disaster. There is no sudden destruction; instead there is ongoing uncertainty, an inability to plan the future and a sense of “there is nothing I can do”.

Financial hardship, unemployment and debt are among the strongest social determinants of mental health. These crises do not affect everyone equally: low-income households, migrants, older people living alone and single-parent families are more vulnerable.

Epidemics and periods of social tension likewise weaken social ties — yet in these periods those very ties are the most protective factor.

02

Expected reactions

  • Constant worry, hopelessness about the future
  • Sleep problems and persistent fatigue
  • Tension at home, impatience, more arguments
  • Shame or a sense of failure; avoiding asking for help
  • Withdrawing from people, losing interest in things once enjoyed

Prolonged stress wears down body and mind; these reactions are not a personal failing but a result of how heavy the circumstances are.

03

What you can do

  • Focus on the small areas you can control: daily routine, sleep, short walks.
  • Break the problem into parts; look into official advice and social services for debt, work or housing.
  • Talk with your family in age-appropriate language — without hiding the situation from children, but without burdening them.
  • Keep up social ties; asking for help is not a weakness.
  • Limit the news feed; stay away from sources that increase uncertainty.

04

When to seek support

  • Reactions last longer than four weeks or keep getting worse
  • Daily tasks — work, school, caring for children — have become impossible
  • Use of alcohol, medication or other substances has increased
  • The person is completely withdrawing and not talking to anyone
  • There are thoughts of harming oneself or others — in this case, without waiting

Do not wait: if danger is ongoing, or you or someone else is thinking about self-harm, call 112 now; for violence call 155. TAK-DER is not a crisis line; see where to turn.
