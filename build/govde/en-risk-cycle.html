Risk and response cycle

Five stages from understanding risk to recovery. The cycle maps onto the four priorities of the UN Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, giving a common language with national and international partners.

  1. 4.1 Understanding risk
  2. 4.2 Prevention and risk reduction
  3. 4.3 Preparedness
  4. 4.4 Response during an event
  5. 4.5 Recovery and reconstruction

4.1

Understanding risk

Psychosocial preparedness is impossible without knowing which hazard affects whom in Cyprus, and how. At this stage each hazard — earthquake, wildfire, flood, violence — is examined separately: the groups most likely to be affected, existing services and gaps, and how the community perceives the risk.

Starting point: the Cyprus risk profile. A detailed analysis of each hazard will be written as separate pages together with the relevant institutions.

4.2

Prevention and risk reduction

Prevention aims to stop a hazard turning into a disaster; risk reduction aims to lessen the impact of risks that cannot be avoided. In psychosocial terms this means strengthening social ties, knowing vulnerable groups in advance and building coordination between institutions.

At this stage the association works through advocacy, partnerships with institutions and research.

Sendai

Priorities 2 and 3 — Strengthening risk governance; investing in risk reduction for resilience

Tools

  • Reducing non-structural risks at home

4.3

Preparedness

Preparedness is addressed at three levels: institutions (psychosocial support plans, trained staff), schools (teacher training, age-appropriate preparedness lessons) and families (family disaster plan, emergency kit, talking with children beforehand).

Sendai

Priority 4 — Preparedness for effective response

Tools

  • Family disaster plan
  • Emergency kit
  • School preparedness curriculum
  • Training and calendar

4.4

Response during an event

During an event the priority is saving lives; psychosocial support is given alongside meeting basic needs, never in conflict with them. Psychological first aid, verified information and reuniting families are the foundation of this stage.

When an event occurs, an incident page is opened on the site from a template prepared in advance: verified information, where to get support, what to do, and updates.

Sendai

Priority 4 — Preparedness for effective response

Tools

  • Psychological first aid
  • Child-friendly space
  • Misinformation and verification

4.5

Recovery and reconstruction

Recovery takes months and years, not weeks. This stage focuses on follow-up, grief support, referring people with long-lasting reactions to specialist care and looking after responders. “Build Back Better” applies not only to buildings but also to social ties and services.
