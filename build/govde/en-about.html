The organisation that is still there after the cameras leave

The association was founded on a simple observation: a disaster's effect on people lasts far longer than the event itself. Across Cyprus we run preparedness, coordination and psychosocial recovery as a single chain of work.

Our purpose

To reduce the harm disasters cause, and to support the psychosocial recovery of those affected through a rights-based, trauma-informed approach.

Our vision

A Cyprus where preparedness is a habit rather than an exception, and mental health support after a disaster is a standard rather than a privilege.

How we work

Six principles, without exception

These principles are set out in Article 3 of our statute and checked against every programme proposal, one by one.

Do no harm comes first

A well-meant but unplanned response can do more damage than no response at all. No team of ours enters the field untrained.

People own their own stories

We never use someone's photograph, name or account without their consent, and we never fundraise on images of suffering.

Local knowledge sits at the centre

We start programme design together with professional chambers, neighbourhood offices and women's cooperatives in the area.

We plug into the system

We do not build a parallel structure; we run our field work in step with the existing relief effort and fill the gaps in it.

Responders get worn down too

Volunteers and staff receive secondary trauma support, and shift limits and mandatory rest are enforced.

Accounts are given, not extracted

Income and spending are published with a per-programme breakdown, and past years' reports are never removed from this site.
