Glossary

Core terms on trauma, disaster and crisis in Turkish and English. The aim is for both communities on the island and international partners to give the same term the same meaning.

22 terms

English · Turkish

Definitions are summarised from the source documents; they are not official translations. Disaster-risk terms follow UNDRR terminology; psychosocial terms follow IASC and WHO documents.

Build Back BetterDaha iyisini yeniden inşa etmek
Using recovery and reconstruction to increase the resilience of communities, rather than merely restoring what was there before. — UNDRR
CapacityKapasite
The combination of strengths, skills and resources available to a community to manage risk and strengthen resilience. — UNDRR
CrisisKriz
A sudden or ongoing situation of severe stress in which a person’s or community’s usual ways of coping are not enough. — WHO / IASC
DisasterAfet
A serious disruption of the functioning of a community, due to a hazard interacting with conditions of exposure, vulnerability and capacity, leading to human, material, economic or environmental losses. — UNDRR
Disaster riskAfet riski
The potential loss of life, injury or damaged assets that could occur in a community in a given period; a function of hazard, exposure, vulnerability and capacity. — UNDRR
Disaster risk reductionAfet riskinin azaltılması
Policies and practices aimed at preventing new disaster risk, reducing existing risk and managing residual risk. — UNDRR
Early warning systemErken uyarı sistemi
An integrated system of hazard monitoring, forecasting and communication that enables people to take timely action. — UNDRR
ExposureMaruz kalma
The situation of people, infrastructure, housing and other assets located in hazard-prone areas. — UNDRR
HazardTehlike
A process, phenomenon or human activity that may cause loss of life, injury, property damage, social and economic disruption or environmental degradation. — UNDRR
Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS)Ruh sağlığı ve psikososyal destek (RSPSD)
The full range of support aiming to protect psychosocial well-being or to prevent and treat mental disorders; described by the four-layer intervention pyramid. — IASC
MitigationRisk azaltma (zarar azaltma)
Lessening or limiting the adverse impacts of a hazardous event. — UNDRR
PreparednessHazırlık
The knowledge, skills and capacities developed in advance to respond to and recover from a likely disaster effectively. — UNDRR
PreventionÖnleme
Activities and measures to avoid existing and new disaster risks. — UNDRR
Psychological first aidİlk psikolojik yardım
Humane, supportive and practical help for a person in distress: noticing needs, listening, comforting, and connecting them with information and services. It is not therapy. — WHO
Psychosocial supportPsikososyal destek
Any local or outside support that protects or promotes psychological well-being and social ties together. — IASC
ReconstructionYeniden yapılanma
The medium- and long-term rebuilding of infrastructure, services, housing and facilities damaged by a disaster, in a more resilient way. — UNDRR
Recoveryİyileşme
Restoring and improving the livelihoods, services and living conditions of a disaster-affected community. — UNDRR
ResilienceDayanıklılık (dirençlilik)
The ability of a system, community or person exposed to a hazard to resist, absorb, adapt to and recover from its effects in a timely way. — UNDRR
ResponseMüdahale
Actions taken directly before, during or immediately after a disaster to save lives, reduce health impacts and meet basic needs. — UNDRR
Secondary traumatic stressİkincil travmatik stres
Stress reactions resembling trauma reactions that arise in a person who witnesses or listens to others’ traumatic experiences. — WHO / IASC
TraumaTravma
An experience of living through, witnessing or learning of a threat to one’s own or another’s life that overwhelms the capacity to cope, and its psychological impact. — WHO
VulnerabilityKırılganlık
The physical, social, economic and environmental conditions that make a person, community or system more susceptible to the impacts of hazards. — UNDRR
