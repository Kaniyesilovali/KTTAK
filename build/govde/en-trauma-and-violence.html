Trauma and violence

A sudden loss, an accident, an assault or ongoing violence: experiences that shake a person’s sense of safety, and what can follow.

On this page

  • What it is
  • Expected reactions
  • What you can do
  • When to seek support
  • References

01

What it is

A traumatic event is one in which a person experiences, witnesses or learns that their own life or someone else’s was in danger. The same event does not leave the same mark on everyone; its severity, the person’s history and the support they receive shape the outcome.

Violence takes many forms: domestic violence, gender-based violence, sexual violence, child abuse and community violence. Where violence is ongoing, safety comes before everything: support only works once the person is safe.

Reactions after trauma are not a sign of weakness; they are the mind and body trying to protect themselves against danger.

02

Expected reactions

  • Trouble falling asleep, waking often, nightmares
  • Startling at sudden noises, feeling constantly on guard
  • Going over the event again and again — or not wanting to think about it at all
  • Restlessness, anger, crying spells, emotional numbness
  • Difficulty concentrating, forgetfulness
  • Headaches, stomach problems, muscle tension, fatigue
  • Guilt, shame or “why did I survive” thoughts
  • Avoiding places, people or conversations that bring back the event

These are normal reactions to an abnormal event. For most people they ease noticeably within a few weeks, with safety, routine and social support.

03

What you can do

  • If someone tells you they have experienced violence: believe them, do not judge, do not press for details.
  • Respect the person’s right to make their own decisions; do not decide for them.
  • Help them make a safety plan: a safe place, a person to contact, important documents.
  • Notice your own feelings and limits; carrying someone else’s burden is tiring.
  • If you suspect a child is being abused, do not investigate alone; report it to the competent authorities.

04

When to seek support

  • Reactions last longer than four weeks or keep getting worse
  • Daily tasks — work, school, caring for children — have become impossible
  • Use of alcohol, medication or other substances has increased
  • The person is completely withdrawing and not talking to anyone
  • There are thoughts of harming oneself or others — in this case, without waiting
  • The person is still living where the violence is happening

Do not wait: if danger is ongoing, or you or someone else is thinking about self-harm, call 112 now; for violence call 155. TAKDER is not a crisis line; see where to turn.
