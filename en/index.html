The first 72 hours matter. So do the next 72 months. KTTAK builds preparedness before a disaster, runs field coordination during one, and follows psychosocial recovery long after — and publishes the accounts for all of it.

How we work

A disaster is not a moment. It is a timeline.

No phase substitutes for another. An organisation that works only on the last one arrives too late; one that works only on the first leaves the job unfinished.

  1. Phase 01 — Before

    Preparedness

    Training in schools, workplaces and neighbourhood units: evacuation planning, family reunification protocols, basic disaster literacy and fire evacuation.

    Preparedness work

  2. Phase 02 — During

    Coordination

    Field teams, needs assessment and volunteer routing, in step with the Civil Defence Organisation and local authorities. Not a parallel structure — a team that plugs into the existing one.

    Field coordination

  3. Phase 03 — After

    Psychosocial recovery

    The work that remains once the cameras leave. Support groups for children and adolescents, grief work, secondary trauma support for responders, long-term case follow-up.

    Psychosocial support

Looking for support, for yourself or someone close to you?

Disrupted sleep, startle responses, avoiding crowds and feelings of guilt are common after a disaster — and you do not have to assume they will pass on their own. Our free helpline is open on weekdays, 09:00–18:00. We keep no records for referrals or appointments.
